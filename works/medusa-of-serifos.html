Large Head of Medusa

Archaeological Museum of Serifos
Historical referencePerseus and Medusa · Iron and copper mined on Serifos since antiquity

Serifos, in the Cyclades, occupies an important place in ancient Greek mythology: Perseus was sent from the island to bring back Medusa’s head. On his return journey, he rescued Andromeda and brought her back to the island.

Because Serifos has been mined for iron and copper since antiquity, Petrides chose to combine these two metals in a monumental head, installed on a concrete column outside the museum.

Project in context

Purpose and setting

The monumental head stands on a concrete column outside the Archaeological Museum of Serifos. Iron and copper connect its finish to the island’s mining history; the Medusa subject connects it to the story of Perseus.

Technical planning pack →

Institution and brief
Created for the Archaeological Collection of Serifos at the invitation of curator Dr. Georgios Gavalas. The artist’s family connection to Serifos and the island’s association with Perseus and Medusa give the work its setting.
Artistic response
The face and wings are finished in copper, the hair in iron and the snakes in aluminium. Oxidized surfaces evoke an object weathered by sea air. The artist’s account identifies Canova’s treatment of Medusa among his sculptural references. The underlying form is 3D-printed in recycled PETG.
Studio and installation
The studio developed and finished the head in New York and supplied installation instructions for its concrete column on Serifos. The instructions describe a separate rear hair section and a fixing arrangement coordinated with the site; final engineering remains specific to the installation.
Scale and documentation
Sculpture height: 75 cm; diameter including hair: 80 cm. The studio archive contains revised installation instructions dated 25 May 2026 and the artist’s project narrative. The column height is not included in these sculpture dimensions.
