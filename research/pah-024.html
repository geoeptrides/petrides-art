Patient responses to the Cleveland Clinic collection

Research record PAH-024 · Finding, limitations, sources and publication relationships.

Back to the research library

PAH-024 · 2014 · United States

Findings, methods and sources

Healthcare artObservational / experimentalReported benefit

Finding. A collection-level survey examined patient-reported mood, stress, comfort and perceptions of care.

Limitation. A self-selected institutional survey with no control condition; do not treat invitation counts as respondent counts or apply results to one sculpture.

Karnik M, Printz B, Finkel J. A hospital's contemporary art collection: effects on patient mood, stress, comfort, and expectations. HERD. 2014;7(3):60-77. Related: Finkel J, Printz B, Gallagher LM, et al. HERD. 2021.

Methods and verification

Verification level: Abstract checked

Structured summary

Population
Members of an online patient panel who had visited Cleveland Clinic’s main campus in the previous 12 months
Sample
Survey sent to 4,376 panel members; respondent number not given in the abstract
Exposure
The hospital’s contemporary art collection
Comparator
None
Outcome measures
Self-reported mood, stress, comfort, satisfaction and impression of the hospital
Timing
Single survey
Result
A majority of respondents noticed the art and reported improved mood and stress and a positive effect on satisfaction
Uncertainty
Self-selected respondents; no control condition
Design
Survey study within an institutional art collection
Sample / scope
See source for sample and design
Setting
Inpatient and public areas
Art form
Art collections
