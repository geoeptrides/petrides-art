Commissioning guidance

An artwork can offer welcome, identity, contemplation or a memorable landmark. Define the purpose before choosing the work.

Planning tools: examples by location, checklist and whole-life budget worksheet

Evaluation guidePrintable board brief

  1. Define the encounter.

    Specify who will see the work, where and for how long. An entrance sculpture, a bedside painting and a mural beside a long waiting area serve different situations.

  2. Develop an artistic brief.

    Set the purpose, desired scale, relationship to architecture, cultural context and budget. Give the artist enough freedom to make a coherent work; specify requirements without promising clinical results.

  3. Include the people who use the place.

    Seek input from patients, families, staff, accessibility specialists and the clinical service. Consultation can improve fit and reveal problems. It does not, by itself, prove that the eventual artwork improves health.

  4. Assess the work in its setting.

    Review sightlines from standing, seated and reclining positions; lighting, glare, circulation and the emotional context of the service. Check fixing, cleaning and access requirements with the hospital’s estates and infection-prevention teams.

  5. Plan ownership and care.

    Record the gift or commission terms, ownership, maintenance responsibility, conservation requirements, insurance, interpretation and photography rights. Agree how relocation and future changes will be handled.

  6. Evaluate the stated purpose.

    A landmark can be studied through wayfinding; a waiting-room work through experience and observed behaviour. Budget for a proportionate evaluation with the relevant professional support. Publish the result, including null or unfavourable findings.

Budget

Consider the full life of the work.

Include fabrication, transport, site preparation, installation, lighting, interpretation, conservation and evaluation. Donations also carry these costs.

Funding routes

Hospital capital budgets, foundations, charities, gifts and eligible public-art programmes provide different routes. Their conditions vary by institution and jurisdiction.

Financial claims

A projected saving from one study is not a dependable estimate for another hospital. Build the financial case on actual project costs and stated institutional purposes.
