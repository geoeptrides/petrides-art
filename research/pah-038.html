Hospital gallery visits after cancer surgery

Research record PAH-038 · Finding, limitations, sources and publication relationships.

Back to the research library

PAH-038 · 2021 · United States

Findings, methods and sources

Adjacent evidenceRandomised trialMixed findingsIncludes null findings

Finding. A randomised study of 80 patients reported benefits for hope, anxiety and mental wellbeing after gallery visits; pain did not significantly differ.

Limitation. Receptive art viewing, but visits also involve movement, attention and a change of setting. This is not an isolated sculpture study.

Lone Z, Hussein AA, Khan H, et al. Art Heals: randomized controlled study investigating the effect of a dedicated in-house art gallery on the recovery of patients after major oncologic surgery. Ann Surg. 2021;274(2):264–270. doi:10.1097/SLA.0000000000004059

Methods and verification

Verification level: Abstract checked

Structured summary

Population
Patients undergoing major oncologic surgery at Roswell Park Comprehensive Cancer Center, Buffalo, New York
Sample
80 patients randomised to two arms
Intervention
Exposure to a dedicated in-house art gallery during the perioperative period (the hospital describes 15-minute daily visits)
Comparator
Standard of care
Outcome measures
Pain Rating Scale; Herth Hope Index; State-Trait Anxiety Inventory; Warwick-Edinburgh Mental Wellbeing Scale
Timing
Post-study questionnaires; exact time points not yet extracted from the full text
Result
Hope +2.4 vs +0.05 points (P = 0.004); anxiety −8 vs −0.9 points (P < 0.0001); mental wellbeing +5.23 vs −0.05 points (P < 0.0001); no significant difference in pain
Uncertainty
P values reported; confidence intervals not given in the abstract; single centre; visits combine art viewing with movement and a change of setting
Design
Randomised controlled trial, single centre
Sample / scope
80 patients
Setting
Post-surgical inpatient care
Art form
Art collections
