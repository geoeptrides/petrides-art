What do we inherit from our families, and what do we choose to pass on?

Six sculptural portraits trace four generations through displacement, care, work and renewal, inviting visitors to consider their own family histories.

Six portraits. Four generations. A Greek family’s story of departure, care and renewal, told through larger-than-life sculpture.

The exhibition at a glance

The exhibition
Six contrasting faces, joined by family ties. Worked surfaces and individual gazes invite visitors to move around the sculptures and look closely.
Available for exhibition
Six sculptures, Arabic and English interpretation, catalogue and supporting films.
For the host
A gallery experience for adults and families, extended through artist-led workshops in portraiture and digital making.

Selected credentials: Tiffany & Co. commissions; Davy Crockett at the Alamo accepted by the White House as a Presidential Gift in 2025. See selected public works.

Connections with Gulf heritage

The family’s story meets the region most directly through the sea: Maria’s departure from Smyrna and Christos’s working life at sea. Family care, respect for elders and the passing of values between generations are experiences visitors will recognise from their own lives.

For host institutions: national vision and regional heritage references.

Artist-led programme: make, discover, discuss

Three proposed activities for the artist’s workshop visit, which can run over several days.

Family portrait workshop
Families · 90 minutes · up to 20. Make a clay portrait or drawing that carries a family memory.
From clay to digital sculpture
Students and adults · 90 minutes · up to 25. Discover how hand modelling, scanning and digital tools shape a sculpture.
Memory and inheritance
Public conversation · 60 minutes. Explore the family’s story with the artist and a locally selected moderator.

Durations and capacities are indicative. Dates, session numbers, materials and interpretation are arranged with the host. Evaluation: attendance, participation and optional visitor or teacher feedback.

The sea: departure, livelihood and belonging

The Mediterranean, the Gulf and the Red Sea share a history of lives shaped by the sea.

Departure
For Maria, the sea marked a break with home and the beginning of a new life.
Livelihood
For Christos, voyages were work: time away from his family to support those at home.
Connection
The region’s maritime heritage — the pearling and trading town of Al Zubarah in Qatar, Historic Jeddah as the Red Sea gateway to Makkah, and Khor Dubai’s merchants’ harbour — offers points of connection with these stories of separation, responsibility and return.

Maritime references: UNESCO World Heritage Centre, Al Zubarah Archaeological Site; Historic Jeddah, the Gate to Makkah; Khor Dubai (UAE Tentative List). Family histories: George Petrides.
