Method & transparency

Methods and inclusion criteria

An editorially curated research resource, updated in dated editions. It is not a systematic review.

Scope

The core subject is receptive exposure to installed visual art in healthcare: murals, paintings, photographs, sculpture and related works. “Passive” describes the absence of art-making; it does not mean viewers are emotionally or intellectually inactive.

Art-making and art therapy are outside the core scope. A guided gallery visit remains receptive viewing, but differs from ambient exposure. Responsive installations, sound, movement and wider landscape design are identified as additional components.

Inclusion and scope rules

The core scope is research on receiving or viewing installed visual art in healthcare. Relevant populations include patients, staff and visitors; relevant outcomes include experience, wellbeing, stress, pain and perceptions of the environment. Eligibility does not depend on a favourable result.

  • Core evidence: studies and reviews of visual art encountered in healthcare settings.
  • Adjacent evidence: museum or gallery viewing, facilitated encounters and multi-component interventions. These are labelled because they differ from ambient exposure.
  • Context: theory, mechanisms, policy, guidance and research-gap records. These are not presented as additional health-outcome trials.
  • Outside the core scope: art-making, art therapy, and activities centred on music, performance or creative participation. Broader retained sources are identified as context rather than direct evidence for installed visual art.
  • Practice records: commissioned or donated artworks and institutional programmes are documented separately from research findings.

These rules describe the repository’s editorial scope. This edition applies them to the inherited collection; it does not represent a new prospective search or a complete census of eligible studies.

What we count

Counts on this site use the units below and are recalculated from the release data for each edition.

  • Research record: one entry in the research library. A record may be a study, a review, a framework, a policy document or an institutional account, and some records summarise the same publication. Research records are therefore not a count of unique studies.
  • Installation record: one artwork, or one ensemble presented as a single work, at one institution. The Miraculous Journey (W-09) is one installation record comprising fourteen sculptures. The same artwork at a second institution would be a second record.
  • Institutional case summary: a narrative account of practice. Some summaries cover grouped institutions, a district or a composite of environments, so 27 summaries are not 27 separate hospital art programmes.
  • Artist: an artist can appear in several records. Jaume Plensa appears in W-03, W-25 and W-32.

Filters and charts can place a record in more than one category, so category totals can exceed the number of records. No count on this site measures effectiveness.

The updated date in the data records the last change to a record. The last full editorial audit, abstract or full-text assessment, and technical website QC are separate activities. The 29 September verification ledger covers all 36 records; its snapshot date is not a new verification of every claim.

Full accessibility remediation of the legacy PDF downloads remains pending. The corresponding HTML pages are the preferred reading and form-filling route.

Source category, claim verification and current status

Each installation record keeps three separate fields.

  • Source category describes the kinds of source the record cites: institution or artist (at least one source from the hospital, its charity or collection, the commissioner or the artist); independent only (press, scholarly or heritage sources); or awaiting institutional confirmation (compiled from Wikimedia Commons and Wikidata). The date shown is the date the category was assigned, not a date on which every detail was checked.
  • Claim verification records which details have been checked against a named source, and when. Most records have not yet been checked claim by claim, and say so.
  • Current status records what is known about whether the work is still in place. Historical sources and photographs do not confirm a present location.

A source category of “institution or artist” does not mean that every detail or the current location has been verified. Where information is missing, records say “not reported”, “not assessed”, “not yet searched” or “no eligible study identified in the documented search”, not “none”. Enquiries to institutions are dated on the record and marked as sent only where the correspondence is retained.

The verification ledger (CSV) lists, for each installation record, the claims published, the source category, what has been checked, when, and what remains uncertain. The data dictionary (CSV) defines the fields and allowed values in the downloadable data.

Images

Photographs are displayed only where a licence or permission is recorded, and the basis is recorded for each image in the installation data. Permission to publish a photograph and permission to reproduce the artwork shown are checked separately. Thirteen photographs whose permission could not be verified were withdrawn on 28 September 2026; their records show a placeholder until permission is recorded.

Editorial policy and founder disclosure

Founder installations are documented as examples of practice. Their inclusion does not provide evidence of health benefits.

Records are selected against the scope rules on this page. Inclusion of research does not depend on a favourable finding, and null and unfavourable results are retained. Corrections are dated in the change log below. The founder’s practice as a sculptor is disclosed on the relevant records and on the About page.

Any future study of a founder installation should be assessed by an independent reviewer under the same criteria as other research, and the artist or donor should not control the analysis or the reporting of unfavourable findings. The installation record and any research record would be kept separate.

Understanding the filters

Study / record type describes the research design or kind of background material. Source format separately identifies preprints and conference abstracts; “Other source formats” is not a peer-review rating. Methods availability flags records with limited reporting; “See record verification” does not imply a full appraisal.

Mixed findings means outcomes or groups differed. Includes null findings means at least one reported comparison found no detectable difference. These labels overlap and apply to the record, not every outcome. Full findings and limitations take precedence over the labels.

Classification

Each record identifies its relevance, evidence type, design, sample or scope, finding and limitation. “Healthcare art” describes relevance to the topic, not a high confidence rating. Reviews, trials, observational studies, theory and institutional accounts answer different questions.

The earlier A–E scale mixed descriptions of the literature, causal evidence and practice. It has been retired from the public display. Original grades remain in the downloadable data as historical metadata, not current ratings. This is not the formal GRADE system.

Counts and overlap

The register contains 78 research records. Original IDs are preserved; PAH-020 was absent in the supplied register. PAH-057 to PAH-076 were added in Edition 4.4 and PAH-077 to PAH-079 in Edition 4.5. Several records refer to the same publication, and reviews may include trials listed separately. The 36 artwork/project records (all documenting placement) and 27 case summaries also overlap. Neither category is a count of health-outcome studies. What we count.

Verification, dates and limits

The repository is a curated update, not a systematic review. The current edition’s changes are listed first in the change log below; earlier edition notes are kept as historical records. Each research record displays its editorial review date and verification status; the publication year is shown separately.

The audit reviewed all existing pages, register records and supplied report sections. It checked key disputed claims against primary or author-institution sources and checked the accessibility of cited URLs. An accessible page does not verify every assertion made about it.

Some sources could not be fully accessed or independently re-extracted. Each research record identifies whether its result received a fresh source check or its original citation was retained. Source links may change, and historical installation records do not guarantee current access.

The material is weighted towards English-language and UK/US sources. No comprehensive global search, duplicate independent screening, formal risk-of-bias assessment or exhaustive census of hospital sculpture is claimed.

Search update · 28 September 2026

This edition adds the first documented search. Three PubMed searches were run on 28 September 2026:

  • S1 (152 results): (artwork*[tiab] OR "visual art"[tiab] OR "works of art"[tiab] OR painting*[tiab] OR mural*[tiab] OR sculpture*[tiab] OR "art installation"[tiab] OR "public art"[tiab]) AND (hospital*[tiab] OR "healthcare environment*"[tiab] OR "health care environment*"[tiab] OR "healthcare setting*"[tiab] OR ward[tiab] OR wards[tiab] OR "waiting room*"[tiab] OR clinic*[tiab]) AND (view*[tiab] OR exposure[tiab] OR displayed[tiab] OR installed[tiab] OR "built environment"[tiab]) NOT ("art therapy"[tiab]) AND 2019:2026[dp]
  • S2 (29 results): ("public art"[tiab] OR "public sculpture"[tiab] OR "street art"[tiab]) AND (health[tiab] OR wellbeing[tiab] OR "well-being"[tiab] OR stress[tiab] OR mood[tiab]) AND 2019:2026[dp]
  • S3 (18 results): ("art"[ti] OR "artwork"[ti] OR "artworks"[ti] OR "paintings"[ti]) AND (hospital[ti] OR patients[ti] OR "healthcare"[ti]) AND (anxiety[tiab] OR stress[tiab] OR pain[tiab] OR satisfaction[tiab] OR wellbeing[tiab]) AND (randomized[tiab] OR randomised[tiab] OR trial[tiab] OR controlled[tiab] OR "quasi-experimental"[tiab]) NOT ("art therapy"[tiab] OR "art-making"[tiab] OR "music"[ti]) AND 2015:2026[dp]

They returned 199 unique records, of which 198 could be retrieved. One editor screened titles and abstracts: 8 were included, 6 were already in the repository, and the rest were excluded, most because they did not concern viewing installed art in healthcare. Seven further records came from sources suggested by readers and were checked against their primary sources. Screening decisions are published in the search log (CSV).

Limits: one database, one screener, English-language search terms and no formal risk-of-bias assessment. This is an update, not a systematic review.

Proposed research-development plan

The next research update should publish its databases, exact search strings, search date, eligibility rules, screening decisions and duplicate-publication checks. A starting search would combine visual art terms (artwork, mural, painting, photograph, sculpture) with healthcare settings (hospital, healthcare, inpatient, outpatient, clinic), with the full syntax adapted and reported for each database. This is a proposed approach, not a search already conducted.

A proposed quarterly review would check new studies, corrections and preprint publication status, with an annual review of the search strategy and coverage. No recurring review process is currently claimed. Independent reviewers have not yet been appointed; names, qualifications and interests should be published when their participation is confirmed.
