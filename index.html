Family memory, carried across generations

George Petrides’s figurative work explores family memory, cultural inheritance and the human experiences carried across generations.

Thalia began with black-and-white photographs of his mother as a young woman, around 1950. Working first in clay, he translated those images into a sculptural portrait.

The story of Thalia

From clay to sculpture

Each work takes shape through a sequence of stages before it is finished.

  1. Clay

    The figure is first modelled by hand in clay.

  2. Scanning

    The clay model is captured by 3D scanning.

  3. Digital sculpting

    The form is developed through digital sculpting.

  4. Fabrication and finishing

    Specialised fabrication, including bronze casting, produces the work, which is then finished.
