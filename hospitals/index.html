Hospitals

Sculpture for hospitals and care homes

10 healthcare projects: 7 installed and 3 in process — to be announced. The installed works include six hospitals in Greece and the Imvriaki Enosi retirement home on Imbros (Gökçeada), Türkiye.

Hospitals are where permanent art matters most. A sculpture in an atrium, a garden or an entrance becomes part of how patients and their families live some of the most intense days of their lives, and it expresses the institution’s values for decades.

Two works developed for hospitals

Egymonousa, “the pregnant woman”: a series of standing figures for maternity hospitals and women’s health, each about 110 cm high and handmade, so that no two are quite alike. It is now in place at Tzaneio, Elena Venizelou, Alexandra and Venizeleio-Pananeio hospitals; Aretaieia, the 220 cm figure at Aretaieion, treats the same subject at over life size.

Kore: the portrait of the artist’s daughter at twelve, gaze lifted, for children’s hospitals. Kore II stands at the General Children’s Hospital of Athens ‘Panagiotis & Aglaia Kyriakou’.

Both are made from PETG recycled from discarded medical packaging, finished in metal coatings and patinas. Works can also be developed specifically for a hospital’s own setting and story.

Institutional reference
“This project was not an easy one. Over three years, it involved much stakeholder consultation, multiple board approvals, several permits from the Hellenic Ministry of Culture, and, of course, fabrication by George to the highest possible technical and artistic standards in his studio in New York. He was involved at every step—flying to Athens for critical meetings, overseeing installation, and speaking at the dedication ceremony. His professionalism and patience throughout the many stages of such a complex project were extraordinary. I recommend him without reservation.”
Prof. Nikolaos Vlahos, MD, PhD, Chairman, 2nd Department of Obstetrics and Gynecology, Aretaieio University Hospital, National and Kapodistrian University of Athens. Letter of 20 June 2025.

Evidence, stated carefully

Research gathered on Public Art & Health suggests that art can improve how patients, staff and visitors experience a hospital; it does not show that a sculpture by itself improves clinical outcomes, and we do not claim it.
