Cleveland Clinic Abu Dhabi: art and regional design references

PAH-077 · 2026 · Japan

Student sculpture maquettes exhibited in a hospital museum

Healthcare artDescriptive surveyContext / synthesis

Finding. A month-long exhibition in March 2025 showed sculpture maquettes by second-year sculpture students at the YHRP museum inside Hiroshima University Hospital. A questionnaire was completed by 37 people: 13 hospital users, 9 companions or family members, 3 hospital staff, 5 students, 5 members of the public and 2 others.

Limitation. A temporary exhibition of maquettes, not permanent installed sculpture. A descriptive questionnaire of a self-selected audience, with no comparison. Findings beyond the abstract and respondent table await a checked translation of the methods and results.

PAH-078 · 2019 · France

Le Louvre à l’Hôpital: statues, reproductions and guided sessions

Adjacent evidenceUncontrolled evaluationReported benefit

Finding. Over one year, four large statues were placed in the hospital gardens and reproductions of paintings were shown in shared areas and patient rooms, alongside 83 guided small-group art discussions attended by 451 patients. Of 201 patients assessed, 160 (79.6%) reported decreased anxiety after the art sessions.

Limitation. A multi-component programme with no comparison group reported in the abstract. The anxiety result follows the guided sessions and cannot be attributed to the statues or to any single component.

Methods and verification

Monsuez JJ, François V, Ratiney R, et al. Museum moving to inpatients: Le Louvre à l’Hôpital. Int J Environ Res Public Health. 2019;16(2):206.

Verification level: Abstract checked

Design

Sample / scope

Setting

PAH-079 · 2026 · United Kingdom

Guided-viewing videos at the National Gallery: a randomised trial

Adjacent evidenceRandomised trialMixed findingsIncludes null findings

Finding. In a pre-registered study of 281 participants and a laboratory follow-up of 33, wellbeing improved from before to after viewing in all conditions. Neither a guided-viewing video nor a mindful-breathing video produced greater overall wellbeing change than the comparison conditions; guided viewing raised positive mood more than the mindfulness video.

Limitation. A museum study of paintings, not permanent sculpture or a hospital. Relevant to whether interpretation adds value to viewing, not to the effect of an installed work.

Methods and verification

Trupp MD, Yin S, Landau R, et al. Evaluating guided viewing strategies to enhance well-being effects of art viewing: a three-arm randomised controlled trial. Sci Rep. 2026;16:28690.
